How it works

a quick start, then the fine print below

Blackout poetry — sometimes called erasure poetry — starts with someone else's words. You take a page of existing text and remove most of it, leaving only a handful of words behind. What's left, read in order, becomes your poem. This tool gives you real pages from real books to work with, and a few ways to do the removing (and un-removing).

  1. Get a page. Land on a random one, search for a specific title or author, or narrow things down by genre and century.
  2. Pick your tool. Blackout to cross words out, highlighter to draw attention to them instead.
  3. Mark up the page. Freehand or straight lines, whichever suits the moment.
  4. Read what's left. The words you didn't cover are your poem.
  5. Save it. Download a PNG of the finished page, portrait or square.

Everything below fills in the details — brush sizes, undo, fixing a stray mark, all of it.

Choosing a page
What's the difference between searching and filtering?

Search picks one exact book — type a title or author and select it from the list, and you'll get pages from that book specifically.

Filters (genre, century) narrow a pool instead — check a few boxes and you'll get a random page from any book that matches. They're separate paths: picking a search result clears any filters, and picking a filter clears the search box.

Do filters apply automatically?

Yes — the moment you click a genre or century chip (or clear them), a new random page loads under the new constraints. No extra "apply" step.

How do I get another page without losing my place?

The ← prev page / another random page from this book / next page → row (it appears both above and below the page) all stay within whatever book you're currently reading. The header's new page button is the wider one — it'll jump to a totally new book if you haven't searched or filtered for something specific.

Marking up the page
What's the difference between blackout and highlighter?

Blackout covers words completely — the classic redacted look. Highlighter is translucent and sits under the text, so the words stay readable but get a wash of color over them. Use whichever fits the poem you're after; nothing stops you from using both on the same page.

What do the brush sizes do?

Blackout has four: a thin ballpoint-pen line, plus small, medium, and large squares — useful for anything from crossing out a single letter to blacking out a whole line. Highlighter uses one fixed size, since it's meant to mark words, not cover them precisely.

Freehand vs. the line tool — what's the difference?

Freehand follows your mouse or finger exactly, for the loose, scribbled look most blackout poetry has. The line tool instead draws a perfectly straight line between two clicks — click once to start, click again to finish. It's handy for cleanly striking through a whole line of text.

Switch between them any time under line drawing type. Started a line by mistake? Press Escape to cancel it before the second click.

Fixing mistakes
I made a stray mark — how do I get rid of it?

A few options, depending on what you need:

Undo last blackout stroke removes your most recent ink mark entirely. Micro-undo removes it one small square at a time, if you only want to trim part of a stroke rather than the whole thing.

Select blackout to remove (or the highlighter equivalent) lets you tap directly on any stroke — even an old one, not just your last — to delete just that one.

Clear all highlights wipes every highlight mark on the page in one go, if you'd rather start that layer over completely.

Why won't the font let me change it anymore?

Once you've made your first blackout stroke, the font locks — changing it mid-poem would shift the text underneath and throw off everything you've already covered. Undo every stroke and it unlocks again. Highlighter marks don't lock the font, since they don't cover the text.

Saving your poem
What's the difference between "save poem" and "share"?

save poem always saves the finished image to your device. On most browsers that's a direct download, no extra dialog — but on an iPhone or iPad it opens the share sheet instead and asks you to tap "Save Image," since that's the only way a website can actually save a photo into your Photos library there. share always opens that same native share sheet, on any device, so you can send the image straight to Messages, AirDrop, or wherever else, rather than just saving it.

What's "save shape"?

Portrait keeps the image's natural height — most pages are taller than they are wide. Square fits everything into a 1:1 frame — but instead of always padding or shrinking, it automatically checks whether splitting the page into two side-by-side halves (like an open book) would waste less space than padding a single tall column would. On a narrow screen, splitting usually wins; on a wide screen, or for a short poem, plain padding usually wins. Either way, the text itself never shrinks — only the layout changes.

Is my poem saved anywhere besides my own device?

No — everything happens right in your browser. Your in-progress poem is kept in your browser's local storage purely so it's still there if you reload the page; nothing gets sent anywhere until you explicitly choose to save or share the finished image yourself.

I saw a warning that my progress isn't being saved — what does that mean?

Some browser settings — private/incognito windows especially — block the local storage this tool uses to remember your in-progress poem between reloads. If you see that warning, your work is still fine as long as you don't reload or close the tab; just save the image when you're done rather than counting on it still being there later.

On a small screen
Where did the sidebar go on my phone?

It's tucked behind the small ▓ button in the bottom corner. Tap it once and three small buttons appear above it: a pen/highlighter switch, undo, and a button shaped like a little open drawer — that one opens the full set of tools (colors, brush sizes, fonts, save shape, all of it) as a full-screen panel. Tap ▓ again to put the quick buttons away.

The pen/highlighter and undo buttons are there for the fast back-and-forth of actually drawing, so you're not opening the full panel for every small adjustment — they stay put once revealed, so after the first tap, switching tools or undoing is back to a single tap each.

Where are the genre and century filters on my phone?

They collapse into a single "Filters" row above the page, with a small number badge showing how many are currently active. Tap it to expand the genre and century chips, same as on a larger screen — they just stay out of the way until you need them.

About the texts
Where do the books come from?

Every text here comes from Project Gutenberg, a volunteer effort that digitizes and freely shares public-domain books. If you end up loving this tool, consider donating to them — none of this exists without their work.
